### The Wienermobile-A Fixture in American Culture 

The Oscar Meyer Wienermobile has become one of the most recognized and loved food icons in the U.S. 

#### A little about the Wienermobile:
- It is a 27 feet long hot dog shaped vehicle
- It's been cruising around state to state since 1936
- The official name for the drivers are "HotDoggers"
- The tailpipe is in the shape of a mustard bottle 
- There are multiple Wienermobiles driving around


#### The Wienermobile Through the Years 

1936- The Original Mobile was Born 
- First appearance was in Janesville, Wisconsin
- Was around 13 ft long 
- Designed by the General Body Company of Chicago 

1940- The Mobile was Updated 
- A Glass Dome was Added 
- Starting touring the East and Midwest portions of the U.S. 
- Model was retired during WW2 due to fuel rationing. 

1950s- Museums, Music, and Another Redesign 
- Updated to include a sunroof and sound system 
- The Gertenslager Model is displayed at the Henry Ford Museum
- Brooks Stevens remodeled to a "wiener in a bun" design

1960s and 70s- Motorhome Era 
- Rebuilt out of a Chevy Motorhome base 
- The Wienermobile went international! 
- The Wienermobile program was put on pause in 1977

Late 1980s-Mid 90s- Modernization has Begun
- Six new Mobiles were built out of Chevy vans
- Each traveled over 200,000 miles across the country 

Mid 90s-Present Day- The New Generation 
- 27 ft long, 11 ft tall, around 8,000 lbs.
- Redesigned to have Wifi, a fridge, and other modern elements 
- Rebuilt on larger GMC or Ford frames. 

2023- Renaming Fiasco 
- The Mobile was renamed the FrankMobile
- Backlash was immediate 
- Oscar Meyer returned to the original name.
